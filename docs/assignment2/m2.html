1. Task Formulation & Data Splitting Protocol

The objective of this project is high-resolution semantic scene parsing for autonomous urban driving. Given an input RGB image \( X \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times H \times W} \), the network predicts a pixel-level dense classification map \( \hat{Y} \in \{0, 1, \dots, 18\}^{H \times W} \) spanning 19 semantic classes (e.g., Road, Building, Sky, Car, Pedestrian, Rider), with unlabeled, out-of-boundary, and vehicle hood pixels assigned to ignore index 255 (excluded from loss computation and evaluation metrics).

City-Level Split & Spatial Leakage Prevention

In urban driving video streams, sequential frames captured within the same geographic municipality exhibit heavy visual correlation (similar architecture, road textures, and weather conditions). To strictly prevent spatial data leakage as mandated by Handbook Section 3.1 & 18, our dataset splitting script (scripts/create_splits.py) enforces strict city-level partitioning:

  • Training Set: 2,481 images from 14 distinct German cities (Aachen, Bochum, Bremen, Cologne, Darmstadt, Dusseldorf, Hamburg, Hanover, Kassel, Monchengladbach, Strasbourg, Stuttgart, Ulm, Weimar).
  • Validation Set: 494 images from 4 held-out cities (Erfurt, Jena, Tubingen, Zurich).
  • Test Set: 500 images from 3 cities of the official validation set (Frankfurt, Lindau, Munster).
Leakage Assertion: The intersection between training and validation municipality sets is strictly empty: len(train_cities ∩ val_cities) == 0.

2. Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA)

In accordance with Handbook Section 19.4 and Section 22 (Semantic Segmentation Focus), extensive exploratory analysis was performed covering region sizes, mask qualities, scene co-occurrences, and split distributions.

2.1 Region & Instance Size Distribution per Class

Key Findings: Object scale varies across more than 4 orders of magnitude. Background "stuff" classes (Road, Sky, Building) dominate large continuous regions exceeding \(10^5\) pixels per scene. Conversely, slender foreground "thing" categories (Pole, Traffic Light, Traffic Sign) frequently occupy fewer than \(10^3\) pixels, highlighting the acute risk of vanishing gradients for small categories in standard pooling architectures.

2.2 Mask Quality & Spatial Ignore Distribution

Key Findings: The mean ignore pixel ratio across training samples is 12.8%. The spatial probability map reveals intense concentration in two areas: (1) The bottom center, where the car hood/windshield of the data recording vehicle is systematically masked out; and (2) Distant horizons where small background clutter cannot be unambiguously annotated. This confirms the necessity of configuring ignore_index=255 in the cross-entropy criterion and confusion matrix calculations.

2.3 Scene Complexity & Class Co-occurrence

Key Findings: Images exhibit high structural complexity, with an average of 11.2 unique semantic classes per scene. The conditional co-occurrence heatmap indicates that vehicles (Car, Bus, Truck) and vulnerable road users (Person, Rider, Bicycle) appear almost exclusively in co-presence with Road and Sidewalk, providing strong contextual priors.

2.4 Class Pixel Distribution Across Splits

Key Findings: Class imbalance is extreme: Road and Building constitute over 50% of valid labeled pixels, whereas tail categories (Train: 0.16%, Motorcycle: 0.12%, Rider: 0.14%) account for less than 0.2% each. Crucially, the logarithmic distribution is strictly preserved across Train, Val, and Test splits, proving that our city-level partition is statistically unbiased.

Baseline: Custom U-Net (`src/models/unet.py`)

Our baseline model is implemented purely in PyTorch. The contraction path consists of 4 encoder stages with base channels = 32, doubling feature channels up to 512 at the bottleneck. The expansion path utilizes ConvTranspose2d(stride=2) followed by concatenation of high-resolution feature maps via skip connections to restore fine-grained spatial boundaries.

Pretrained Model: DeepLabV3-ResNet50 (`src/models/deeplabv3.py`)

For our modern pretrained architecture, we employ DeepLabV3 with a ResNet-50 backbone pre-trained on ImageNet-1K (weights_backbone=ResNet50_Weights.IMAGENET1K_V1). To stabilize training and prevent catastrophic forgetting under a limited epoch budget, the entire ResNet-50 feature extractor is frozen (param.requires_grad = False). Only the Atrous Spatial Pyramid Pooling (ASPP) module and the 19-class linear classifier are fine-tuned using AdamW with learning rate \(10^{-3}\).

Quantitative Analysis:
  • Superiority of Transfer Learning: DeepLabV3 with ImageNet pre-training achieves 43.85% mIoU in Epoch 1, immediately outperforming the baseline U-Net after all 10 epochs (31.34% mIoU).
  • Peak Performance Gain: DeepLabV3 peaks at 56.57% mIoU and 69.95% Dice Score at Epoch 9. This represents an absolute gain of +25.23% mIoU (+80.5% relative boost) and +31.75% Dice over the baseline U-Net.
